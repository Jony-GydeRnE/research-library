LATEX HARDENING (these are the ones that break MathJax most often)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Matrix and aligned environments must use \begin{...} ... \end{...}, never bare ampersands inside inline math. Use \begin{aligned} for multi-line equations inside \[ ... \].
- Cases / piecewise: \begin{cases} a & b \\ c & d \end{cases}. Each row separated by \\, columns separated by &.
- Derivatives: prefer \frac{d}{dt} f(t) over df/dt in display. Use \partial for partials. \dot{x} and \ddot{x} for time derivatives are fine.
- Norms and absolute values: \lVert x \rVert and \lvert x \rvert beat \|x\| and |x| (which break in some renderers). Inline |x|^2 is acceptable in body prose.
- Greek letters: \phi, \varphi are different characters; use the one Rodina uses. Same for \epsilon vs \varepsilon. When in doubt, prefer the variant Rodina uses in GROUND_TRUTH.
- Tensor indices: contravariant superscripts and covariant subscripts both need braces when multi-character: x^{\mu\nu}, not x^\mu\nu (which renders as x^μ ν).
- Sums and integrals: \sum_{i=1}^{n} and \int_{a}^{b} with both bounds braced.
- Operators that are words (sin, cos, log, lim, max, sup, inf, ker, im, dim, deg, rank, Tr, det, sgn): use the predefined \sin, \cos, \log, \lim, \max, \sup, \inf, \ker, \dim, \deg, \rank, \Tr, \det, \sgn — never type them as plain letters which render in italic.
- Spacing inside math: \, for thin, \; for medium, \quad for one em, \qquad for two. Avoid \hspace{Xpt} — pixel widths are fragile; use the named widths.
- Dots: \ldots for low dots in math sequences (a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n), \cdots for centered dots between operators (a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n), \vdots for vertical, \ddots for diagonal.
- Always close subscripts and superscripts with braces when content is multi-character: x_{i+1}^{2n} not x_i+1^2n.

DEFINITION-FIRST RULE
---------------------
When a passage introduces a new mathematical object, lead with the definition equation and follow with the prose explanation, NOT the other way around. Rodina's voice puts the math first and the words second. Prefer:

  <p>The Lagrangian is</p>
  <div class="math-display">\[ L = \tfrac{1}{2} m \dot{x}^{2} - V(x) \]</div>
  <p>the kinetic energy minus the potential energy.</p>

Over the wordy version that buries the equation:

  <p>The Lagrangian, which is the kinetic energy minus the potential energy, is given by the formula L equal to one half m x-dot squared minus V of x.</p>
